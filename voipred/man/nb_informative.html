Informative prior of bayesDCA (threshold-varying)

Description

The informative prior of bayesDCA (Cruz and Korthauer, 2025), for a binary outcome: Beta priors on the sensitivity and specificity of each model that change with the threshold, and a Beta prior on the prevalence. It reproduces bayesDCA’s threshold-varying prior (dca(threshold_varying_prior = TRUE)), with the same defaults. Give it to nb_val() as prior = nb_informative(); the value-of-information functions then use it too.

Usage

nb_informative(
  sensitivity = c(0.99, 0.01),
  strength = 5,
  ignorance = c(0.25, 0.75),
  ignorance_prior = c(1, 1),
  prevalence = c(1, 1)
)

Arguments

sensitivity Prior mean of the sensitivity at the lowest and at the highest threshold. Default c(0.99, 0.01).
strength Prior strength (in patients) of the sensitivity and specificity priors at the lowest and highest thresholds. Default 5.
ignorance The vague region, as fractions of the highest threshold: default c(0.25, 0.75). NULL: no vague region.
ignorance_prior The Beta prior (shapes \((a, b)\)) of the sensitivity in the vague region. Default c(1, 1) (uniform: prior mean 0.5, strength 2).
prevalence The Beta prior (shapes \((a, b)\)) of the prevalence. Default c(1, 1) (uniform).

Details

The logic. Under the rule "treat if the predicted risk is at or above the threshold", every model treats everyone at a threshold of 0 (sensitivity 1, specificity 0: treat all) and no one at a threshold above all predicted risks (sensitivity 0, specificity 1: treat none). Near the ends of the decision curve few patients are untreated (low thresholds) or treated (high thresholds), so the data say little there, and a vague prior lets the net benefit, and the value-of-information measures, swing widely. This prior states, with little weight, that the model behaves like treat all at the lowest threshold and like treat none at the highest, and stays vague in between. Cruz and Korthauer (2025) found that such a prior keeps EVPI decreasing with the sample size in small samples, where it otherwise may not.

The construction (as in bayesDCA). Each Beta prior is written as a prior mean \(\mu\) and a prior strength \(s\) (worth \(s\) patients): Beta\((\mu s, (1 - \mu) s)\). With \(t_{min}\) and \(t_{max}\) the lowest and highest thresholds given to nb_val() and the vague ("ignorance") region from ignorance[1] \(\times t_{max}\) to ignorance[2] \(\times t_{max}\), the prior mean of the sensitivity is sensitivity[1] at \(t_{min}\), falls linearly to the mean of ignorance_prior at the start of the vague region, stays there through it, and falls linearly to sensitivity[2] at \(t_{max}\); its strength falls linearly from strength to that of ignorance_prior and rises back to strength in the same way. Without a vague region (ignorance = NULL) the mean falls linearly from sensitivity[1] to sensitivity[2] across all thresholds, with strength strength. Prior means are kept within [0.001, 0.999], and thresholds are taken within [1e-9, 0.99], as in bayesDCA. The prior of the specificity has mean 1 minus that of the sensitivity and the same strength. The priors at different thresholds are independent; each model has the same prior. The prevalence has the Beta prior prevalence at every threshold.

The prior at a threshold depends on the thresholds given to nb_val() (through \(t_{min}\), \(t_{max}\) and the vague region), as in bayesDCA. It is for binary outcomes only, without case weights. With reasonably large samples (e.g. 100 events or more) it changes the results little.

Value

A prior, an object of class nb_prior, for nb_val().

References

Cruz GNF, Korthauer K. Bayesian decision curve analysis with bayesDCA. Statistics in Medicine 2025.

See Also

nb_priors, nb_val()

Examples

library("voipred.dev")

nb_informative()
Prior: informative (bayesDCA): threshold-varying Beta priors on sensitivity and specificity, sensitivity mean 0.99 at the lowest threshold to 0.01 at the highest, strength 5 at the ends, Beta(1, 1) from 25% to 75% of the highest threshold; prevalence Beta(1, 1) 
df_dev <- gusto[1:5000, ]
df_val <- gusto[5001:5500, ]
fit <- glm(day30 ~ age + Killip + sysbp + pulse, family = binomial, data = df_dev)
df_val$p <- predict(fit, newdata = df_val, type = "response")
res <- nb_val(df_val, "p", "day30", thresholds = seq(0, 0.3, by = 0.01),
              prior = nb_informative())
res
