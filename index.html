I'm a machine learning researcher interested in trustworthy machine learning, natural language processing, and probabilistic machine learning. I want to build ML systems whose predictions can be trusted: models that are evaluated honestly under biased or missing labels, that know how uncertain they are, and whose outputs are grounded in evidence. Previously, I worked with Dr. Mohammad Mahdavi at GISMA University of Applied Sciences on multimodal LLMs and retrieval-augmented generation for fact-checking (IEEE ICDM 2025).

Research

I currently work in three directions:

  • Trustworthy ML. Reliable evaluation of models under biased, censored, or missing labels, and decision-theoretic measures of whether a model is actually useful.
  • NLP and LLMs. Grounding LLM outputs in retrieved evidence, including multimodal retrieval-augmented generation for claim verification.
  • Probabilistic ML. Bayesian inference, uncertainty quantification, value of information, and latent-variable models for censored data.

Representative papers are highlighted.

Robust Decision Curve Analysis for Survival Risk Prediction Models in the Presence of Informative Censoring
Amirhosein Farahmand, Mohsen Sadatsafavi, Abdollah Safari, Tae Yoon Lee
SMDM Annual Meeting, Oslo, 2026 Oral
Under review at Statistics in Medicine
abstract / code

Standard evaluation breaks when labels are missing not at random. Using conditional inverse probability of censoring weighting, we estimate time-dependent net benefit under informative censoring, so comparisons between ML risk models stay reliable on biased, incompletely observed labels, for both standard and competing-risk outcomes. The method is contributed to the dcurves R package.

Ongoing Work

voipred: Bayesian Value of Information for Validating ML Models
University of British Columbia & Maastricht University, 2026 – present

An R package that integrates Bayesian and frequentist net-benefit estimation to quantify uncertainty in model validation, with value-of-information measures (EVPI, EVSI) that tell you how much more data is worth collecting before a model is trusted.
Survival-Aware Functional Autoencoders
Marquette University, 2026 – present

A functional autoencoder that generalizes functional PCA, trained with a joint objective of reconstruction loss and the Cox partial likelihood so the latent representation keeps survival-relevant structure. Currently extending it to competing risks and releasing it as an R package.
