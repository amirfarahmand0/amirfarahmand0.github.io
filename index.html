Research

My research interests are trustworthy machine learning, natural language processing, and probabilistic machine learning.

I want to build ML systems whose predictions can be trusted:

  • evaluated reliably under biased, censored, or missing labels,
  • aware of how uncertain they are, and
  • grounded in evidence.

Representative papers are highlighted.

Robust Decision Curve Analysis for Survival Risk Prediction Models in the Presence of Informative Censoring
Amirhosein Farahmand, Mohsen Sadatsafavi, Abdollah Safari, Tae Yoon Lee
SMDM Annual Meeting, 2026  (Oral Presentation)
Under review at Statistics in Medicine
abstract / code

Using conditional inverse probability of censoring weighting, we estimate time-dependent net benefit when labels are missing not at random, so comparisons between risk models stay reliable for both standard and competing-risk outcomes. Contributed to the dcurves R package.

Fact-Checking Sustainability Objectives Using Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation
Mohammad Mahdavi, Amirhosein Farahmand, Abolfazl Nadi
IEEE International Conference on Data Mining (ICDM), 2025
paper / code

A multimodal RAG pipeline in which LLMs retrieve evidence from text and images in a domain-specific knowledge base, reason across both modalities, and decide whether a claim is supported.

A Q-Learning Approach to Maintenance Policy Optimization for Series Systems with Degradation and Common Shocks
Amirhosein Farahmand, Fatemeh Fartash, Mohammadreza Yadollahi, Sina Zarandi
11th Seminar on Reliability Theory and Its Applications, 2025
proceedings / code

Reinforcement learning (Q-learning) for finding optimal maintenance policies in series systems with gamma degradation and common shocks.

voipred: Bayesian Value of Information for Validating ML Models
University of British Columbia & Maastricht University, 2026 – present
code

An R package that combines Bayesian and frequentist net-benefit estimation to quantify uncertainty in model validation, with value-of-information measures (EVPI, EVSI) that say how much more data is worth collecting before a model is trusted.

Survival-Aware Functional Autoencoders
Marquette University, 2026 – present

A functional autoencoder that generalizes functional PCA, trained jointly on reconstruction loss and the Cox partial likelihood so the latent representation keeps survival-relevant structure. Includes an extension to competing risks and an R package implementation.
